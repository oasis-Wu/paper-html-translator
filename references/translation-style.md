# Translation style

## Fidelity

Translate the complete scholarly text into Chinese without summarizing, expanding, fact-checking into the prose, or altering the authors' degree of certainty. Preserve paragraph order, heading depth, numbering, cross-references, citations, footnote markers, and all local asset paths.

## Terminology

- Use established Chinese disciplinary terminology where it is unambiguous.
- At the first meaningful occurrence of a specialized or potentially ambiguous term, use `中文译名（English term）` when that aids verification; use the chosen Chinese term consistently afterward.
- Keep gene symbols, chemical formulae, model identifiers, variable names, abbreviations, SI units, DOI strings, URLs, and accession identifiers unchanged unless a conventional localized form is required.
- Preserve Latin binomials and mathematical notation.
- Do not force a Chinese translation for a proper noun when doing so would make identification harder.

## Figures, tables, and equations

- Reuse the exact image path from the English Markdown.
- Translate figure captions, table titles, column labels, and notes when they are part of the article text.
- Preserve figure/table numbers and in-text references.
- Do not translate equation symbols, variable names, or equation numbers.
- Preserve raw MathML, LaTeX, and HTML table markup unless an equivalent edit can be made without semantic loss.

## Front and back matter

- Translate the article title, abstract, keywords, affiliations' descriptive components, acknowledgements, funding statements, conflict declarations, and data/code availability statements.
- Keep author names recognizable and do not invent Chinese transliterations.
- Keep bibliography entries in their original language by default. Translate only surrounding headings or explanatory notes.
- Preserve journal names, publisher names, DOI values, and URLs.

## Quality review

Verify section-by-section correspondence between the English and Chinese files. Check negation, comparatives, statistical direction, confidence language, numerical values, units, citation groups, figure/table callouts, and terminology consistency. A fluent sentence is not acceptable if it changes the scientific claim.
